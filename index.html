01 Local AI Xeon E5-2680 v4 · 2× Tesla V100 32 GB

Fully local LLM inference stack — open-source models served on a pair of Tesla V100 32 GB cards by llama.cpp and the Strata engine, wrapped in a Hermes agent layer with a custom Discord frontend. Every measured run, table and write-up is published in the repo: github.com/EamonMcKiernan05/Local-LLM-Testing.

Stack
  • 2× Tesla V100 32 GB linked at PCIe 3.0 ×16 — driver 580 pinned as the last Volta driver branch, CUDA 12.9, and llama.cpp rebuilt sm_70-only (144 embedded sm_70 cubins); the Strata engine built for sm_70 serves a 128K-context Qwen3.8-Flash-Next with MTP speculative decoding
  • Extended the box to NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Bolt 74B the day after upstream support merged: rebuilt llama.cpp from master to build 11700 (CUDA 12.9, sm_70 cubins) and served the 74B MoE at 262,144 ctx across both V100s with NVIDIA's separate 1.1B LiLiCorr drafter on llama.cpp's dflash speculative path — 128 of 490 draft tokens accepted and 53 tok/s decode on a prose prompt, against 139 tok/s on a counting test where drafting hits its ceiling
  • A third engine, Project Maya — GLM-5.3-Flash, a ~320B Mixture-of-Experts (~18B active) layer-split across both cards. The newest quant is a 313B-parameter, hand-mixed 87 GB IQ2_XXS whose publisher prunes the MTP block, so it benches with speculative drafting off: at the full 262,144-token window it holds 22.5–24.9 tok/s decode and 714 tok/s prefill at 20K rising to 870 at 200K (851 at 250K), every figure the mean of three fresh-load runs per depth, and engine v1.1.13 → v1.1.15 lifted prefill 7.6–10.3% at every depth while decode stayed inside its run-to-run spread. That window fits because the cache is streamed: a 32,768-position slice of each attention layer stays in VRAM (0.37 GB across both cards) while the other 88% of the 262K positions sit in 2.95 GB of pinned, device-mapped host RAM, and 93–99% of block reads still hit VRAM. Halving the window to 131K changes nothing measurable (+0.3% prefill / +2.6% decode at the shared depths) — long context is essentially free on this box. The Maya-S 321B quant, MTP drafting on, reads 20.4–22.6 tok/s decode and 595–646 tok/s prefill. Moving the model set off a DRAM-less boot SSD onto a dedicated NVMe was worth +23.9% decode / +46.0% prefill mean on a same-day, three-runs-per-side controlled pair.
  • Measured speculative decoding on that quant instead of assuming it pays: the GLM-5.3-Flash DFlash2 drafter (Q4_K_M, 697 MB, five layers) accepted 63% of its drafts and still ran 27–31% slower than the same engine without it — 15.9–18.0 tok/s decode against 22.5–24.9 — so drafting stays off for this model
  • Two-card tensor split with MTP; the full 262,144-token window runs end to end — a 250,632-token prompt prefilled at 1,495 tok/s, then 6,000 tokens generated at 48.8 tok/s
  • Cooling rebuilt around a single 7k-rpm PWM fan driven by a systemd hwmon fan curve — a ten-minute soak of six back-to-back 100k-token prefills held dead-flat at 61–62 °C (1,078 tok/s, 0.7 tok/s spread). Redesigned the 80 mm ducting into a mirrored one-fan-per-card pair — each fan 30 mm outboard of the stack, the cards’ original one-slot (40 mm) mouth spacing kept, and both meshes closed and single-body on export
Current Numbers (October 2026)
  • Engine v0.1.32 → v0.1.39, same model and config byte-for-byte: decode +22–31% at every depth (20k: 56.6 → 73.9 tok/s; 250k: 50.3 → 65.0) and prefill +23–26% from 60k up (peak 1,973 tok/s at 100k)
  • 126-run MTP sweep against the live server across all six depths: gate 0.50 confirmed fastest and the stock configuration measured fastest overall, with 250k-prompt prefill flat at 1,494–1,498 tok/s
  • model-bench harness for any OpenAI-compatible endpoint — browser UI + CLI, auto-detects llama.cpp or Strata, writes per-run JSONL/CSV/markdown; published at github.com/EamonMcKiernan05/Local-AI-Speed-Test-Harness
  • Enabled vision on the base model — the image encoder built locally for sm_70, verified with a real image read in 4.0 s at 93 tok/s decode
  • One-shot demo bench — single-prompt browser demos written by the local model: run them all in the demo gallery

06 AI Trading Bot & Backtesting Python · Binance · Walk-Forward · Docker + s6

AI-agent-driven trading system in two phases: a rigorous backtesting methodology (mechanical trend-following rules, realistic cost modelling, out-of-sample validation), then a production trading bot that ran the validated strategy live on Binance for 22 days — built to test whether disciplined rules beat buy-and-hold on real 2026 market data.

Backtesting Engine
  • Python backtesting engine over real daily OHLC (Binance crypto, Yahoo stocks) with realistic costs: taker fees, funding-rate accrual, and overnight stock financing
  • Blind backtest: strategy rules committed to a written receipt before inspecting any price data — six sleeves (BTC/ETH/SOL 10x perp, NVDA/TSLA 5x long, COIN 5x short), daily hard stops, weekly trend filters, and earnings blackouts
  • Result: £100 → £135.85 (+35.9%) over ~8 months vs −14.5% for equal-weight buy-and-hold of the same six assets — the edge came from trend filters keeping capital out of three crashing crypto assets
Validation
  • Top-50 crypto universe swept across 120 parameter combinations, then walk-forward validation: tuned on Jan–Apr, ran the single best config untouched on the unseen May–Aug window
  • Out-of-sample +16.5% vs +0.6% buy-and-hold — confirming the result was not a curve-fit artefact; all top-8 train configs clustered within £1.20 on the test window (robustness check)
  • Live paper trading: 10x BTC perpetual, +146.5% on £10 margin (take-profit exit), with full trade log and daily equity curve retained
Live Trading Bot
  • Deployed the walk-forward-validated top-3 strategy (BTC/ETH/SOL, spot, long-only, no leverage) as a 24/7 production bot on a dedicated Debian LXC on the Proxmox cluster: SMA20 trend filter, 8% trailing stop, 3% fixed stop checked live on every 5-minute poll
  • Deterministic rules engine in pure Python (stdlib + requests only) — no improvisation: persistent state, unit tests, and a backtest-reproduction test asserting the validated engine’s exact £129.24 outcome (±0.5%)
  • Binance REST integration with a hard dry-run gate: real order code implemented but disabled until go-live; keys never committed (gitignored, chmod 600, wired only at go-live)
  • Single Docker image appliance: s6-overlay supervises the bot loop and a read-only web dashboard (GET-only, no exchange import), persistent data on a host bind mount, non-root
  • AI analysis bridge: trade events call a Hermes trading profile via API server; autonomous monitoring — a daily 06:00 agent-mode review pulls bot state and independently cross-checks live Binance prices (positions, armed stops, stop breaches, 7-day P&L vs buy-and-hold), plus a monthly review against the walk-forward baseline with an explicit strategy/investment verdict; documented go-live checklist
  • Went live with real funds (September 2026): first BTC order filled on Binance, then root-caused and fixed two live-only bugs — exchange info cached for the first symbol only (ETH/SOL rejected by LOT_SIZE) and MARKET fills recording a zero price — plus container-timezone and real-account-equity tracking fixes, all verified against the live API
  • Hardened the read-only dashboard after a live review of its charts: the klines endpoint was asking Binance for a single page (its 1,000-candle hard limit), so a 7-day per-minute view actually began just after midnight. The window is now walked backwards until it is covered or a 12,000-row cap is reached, and a granularity that cannot cover the chosen range says so in the hint line instead of quietly drawing less. Axis ticks moved onto natural steps (1/5/15/30 min through to 28 days) at round clock times, with the date added once a window crosses a day, and the page now fills the window rather than capping at 1200 px. Also fixed a destroyed-chart bug where one failed kline fetch left a dead reference that threw on the next resize and blanked the whole dashboard until a page reload — a transient exchange error could take monitoring down for the day; failures are now caught per section and self-heal on the next 60-second refresh
  • Ran live for 22 days on real funds (6–28 September 2026): funded $38.26, finished flat at $38.25 (−0.03%), then wound down deliberately — final real-account state snapshotted, the container stopped and verified unreachable, and auto-restart disabled so a reboot could not bring it back
Decision-Model Evaluation (September 2026)
  • Screened a hosted decision model (TypeSafe’s Jev-1.13, a typed decision API rather than a chat model) as a candidate trading brain: a ten-cadence replay of the live bot’s own three-week window plus a three-month 5-minute replay — 113,000+ calls, 0.32–0.38s mean latency, ~$4.50 in total API spend. It underperformed buy-and-hold of the same three coins in both windows and acted on just 0.07–0.08% of the questions asked, so it stayed out of production.
  • Established the real break-even from Binance’s live order filters: at a $10 stake a BTC round trip needs a +8.65% move against a 0.25% mean hourly move (lot-step flooring and the $5 minNotional strand cash permanently), and lot-step dust alone stranded 13.2% of the sleeve across the three-month replay — at these stakes the binding cost is exchange mechanics, not the model.
  • Validated forward before trusting it: an 11-hour live paper run (1,993 calls, ten books at 1–10 minute cadences on real prices, $0.07 of spend) took one trade per book at near coin-flip confidence, and the negative result was written up with a corrections section, stated limits and a re-runnable harness rather than shelved — the live account kept its walk-forward-validated rules.
  • Extended the evaluation to its full window: a seven-month replay (Jan–Jul 2026, 61,056 five-minute ticks per book) at $60 and then $100 book sizes, the larger pass running four books side by side — control and rich state under the live mechanics and under a fee-aware buy guard — 243,797 Jev calls and $10.80 of spend, completed after a mid-run re-fill when the API provider's account-level budget cap cut the first pass short. It surfaced a genuine execution-mechanics defect: every sell floors to Binance's lot step, so a position bought with the last ~$5 of cash sits under the $5 minimum order once the fee is taken and can never be sold — the rich-state book under the live mechanics froze again, dead from May with three positions it could never sell (the same defect froze both $60 books), while the guarded book ended the window with zero trapped positions.
  • The verdict held at both book sizes and with the fix: in a window where the three coins fell 28–42%, no book beat buy-and-hold once unsellable lot-step dust was excluded — $12–35 of sellable value against $63.45 for simply holding, and the heaviest-churn book paid $12.72 in fees plus $70.82 stranded as unsellable dust across its 339 round trips.
  • Stress-tested whether a confidence gate could rescue it — offline, against the stored answers, so no new API calls: a gate sweep from 0.50 to 0.85, then a full buy×sell mixed grid. The higher gates "won" only by trading less (≥0.70 left 1 and 5 round trips across the seven months, mostly cash at $93.07 / $88.67), and the mixed grid showed no consistent sell-side effect — the flip-flopping signature of small-sample noise, not signal: 78% of the executed round trips came from 50–60% confidence buys at a 32% win rate, and only 16 buys in the entire window cleared P≥0.70. Read as what it is: the gate is an off-switch, not alpha.
  • Closed out the start-mode and rule-guard questions — a final pre-invested run plus offline sweeps against the stored answers: the pre-invested book ($33/coin bought at the 1 Jan close, managed from tick 0) finished at $66.25 of $99 against $63.79 for never trading, and a full cash-vs-invested gate grid confirmed every winning cell won by not participating. Hard rule overrides — take-profit, dip-exit with a re-entry lockout, and a trailing stop on each position's own peak with a rebound re-entry — all helped in the falling window (best settings: $66 → $96 against $63.79 for holding) and all lost in the rising one (the tightest guard turned the rise's $45 into $31, while doing nothing made $48): protection in a fall is the whipsaw tax in a rise.
Further Strategy Studies (September 2026)
  • Tested the "hold while rising, cash while falling" filter family across five speeds (SMA20–SMA200 plus a 2% buffer band) as pre-committed rules on 2017→2026 daily closes: in the modern window it did not beat holding — pooled $300: best filter $1,724 vs $1,917 for buy-and-hold — while roughly halving drawdowns (BTC −53% → −32%, ETH −68% → −38%, SOL −76% → −58%) and dodging nearly the whole 2026 fall; the full-history version where the same rules win big ($5.6k–$29.4k vs $1.9k) is arithmetic from dodging 2018/2022, a structurally different era. Whipsaws were real — six switches in five weeks at BTC's late-2025 top — and a one-day execution lag moves finals 20–40%: recorded as crash insurance, not a wealth tool.
  • Encoded two "volume profile" setups from a trading thread — a value-area-low fakeout bounce and a thin-zone breakout — as pre-committed rules on cached 1-minute bars across matched falling and rising windows: 587 round trips with a weighted expectancy of −0.056% per trade before costs (a coin flip), the entire loss coming from fees ($95.86) and lot-size stranding ($161.42); 11 of 12 books ended below their $100 start, all 36 sensitivity cells negative, and the thread's advertised 80% win rate was not in the data.
Forward Paper-Testing Tracks (September 2026)
  • Built three supervised forward paper-testing tracks — crypto, US stocks and Polymarket — all driven by Jev and paper-only end to end (no keys, no orders anywhere): a crypto track (two state variants over a 21-day window: a control replicating the earlier one-day test, plus a richer state carrying code-computed RSI-14, EMA-20/50, 15/60/240-minute changes, volume deltas and Jev's own last three answers per coin — first swept across ten decision cadences, then settled to a single 5-minute cadence once the replays had shown interval choice was noise); a stocks track (20 liquid US large caps, events from EDGAR filings — 8-K full text, 10-Q/10-K metadata — plus a daily per-ticker news digest, across filings-only, filings+news and price-state control books); and a Polymarket track (a daily scan of ~150 top open markets judged for resolution clarity, news staleness and P(yes), trading divergence across three books with the gates added one at a time).
  • Every track trades on the real market mechanics: Binance lot-size flooring with sub-$5 dust stranded and never sellable on crypto; whole-share flooring at a $500 clip with IBKR commission max($0.35, 0.35% of shares) per side on stocks; Polymarket's per-category taker-fee formula on $25 positions — max 10 open per book, resolutions within 30 days, held to settlement.
